Student Visa Requirements for the UK in 2026: New Rules Every Applicant Must Know

By Amara DialloPublished June 18, 2026⏳ 12 min read

UK Student Visa refusals follow predictable patterns. Bank statements that show large transactions outside the standard assessment window. Financial documents that are technically sufficient but formatted in ways that raise questions. Applications submitted without understanding that the Home Office is not just checking whether the money exists: it is checking whether the money has been sitting in the account long enough, in the right form, with a clear enough paper trail to satisfy a standardized review process. Understanding exactly what that process is looking for, before submitting, is the difference between approval and a refusal that costs both time and money to challenge.

The Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS)

The first thing you need for a UK Student Visa is a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS) number from your university. The CAS is a reference number the university sends you after you've accepted a conditional or unconditional offer and met all conditions (including any English language requirements). You cannot apply for a UK Student Visa without a CAS. The visa application window opens six months before your course start date, but you can only apply once you have your CAS.

Your CAS contains information about your course, the university, and the fees you've paid. Make sure all the details match your actual offer letter and fee payment. Discrepancies between your CAS and supporting documents are a common cause of delay.

Financial Requirements in 2026

You need to show you can cover both tuition fees and living costs. The financial requirements were updated in 2024 and remain in force for 2026. You need:

  • One year of tuition fees (as stated in your CAS) if you haven't already paid them
  • Living costs maintenance funds: £1,334/month for up to nine months if studying in London (£12,006 total), or £1,023/month outside London (£9,207 total)

If you're applying for less than nine months, the maintenance amount is multiplied by the course length in months. These funds must have been in your bank account continuously for at least 28 days before your visa application date - this is the rule my friend fell afoul of. The 28-day window must end no more than 31 days before your application date. Set up a calendar reminder the moment you decide to apply.

English Language Requirements

UK Student Visas require proof of English proficiency from a Home Office-approved Secure English Language Test (SELT) provider. The approved tests are IELTS for UKVI (not standard IELTS Academic), TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic, and Trinity ISE. Standard IELTS Academic scores are not accepted for visa purposes - it must specifically be IELTS for UKVI, taken at an approved test center. The minimum required score for most universities is IELTS 6.0 overall, but your specific university and course may require higher scores.

The exemption most guides omit: if you're a national of a majority English-speaking country (including the US, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and several others), or if your previous degree was taught entirely in English at a UK-recognized institution, your university may be able to waive the English test requirement for CAS purposes - though UKVI still requires the SELT score for the visa application. Check with your university's international admissions office specifically.

The Graduate Visa: Post-Study Work Rights in 2026

The UK Graduate Visa, introduced in 2021, allows international students who complete a qualifying degree (bachelor's or higher) to remain in the UK and work for two years after graduation (three years for PhD graduates). You can work for any employer in any role - there's no sponsorship requirement. This is one of the most significant changes to UK post-study work rights in a generation, and it appreciably improves the value proposition of UK study for career-focused international students. Apply within the UK within the six months before your student visa expires.

New Restrictions in 2024/25 That Remain in Force

Several restrictions introduced in 2024 under the previous UK government are still in effect in 2026. International students can no longer bring dependant family members on a Student Visa unless they're enrolled in a government-sponsored scholarship, a postgraduate research program, or a higher education program that is government-sponsored. The dependant restriction doesn't affect the main applicant but affects students who planned to bring spouses or children. International students also cannot switch to most immigration routes from within the UK while still on a Student Visa - you must complete your course first and then apply for a Graduate Visa or another route.

Common Refusal Reasons and How to Avoid Them

The most frequent refusal reasons for UK Student Visas: funds not continuously held for 28 days, bank statement dates falling outside the valid window, inconsistencies between CAS information and supporting documents, inadequate English language test scores, and concerns about genuine student intent. The last one - genuine intent - is where refusals can feel arbitrary. UKVI looks at whether your stated reasons for studying in the UK are plausible, whether your planned course relates to your previous education and career path, and whether your financial situation supports your plans. Be consistent and specific in your application.

The Application, Step by Step

The sequence matters, because several steps depend on the one before and doing them out of order wastes weeks.

One. Receive an unconditional offer from a licensed student sponsor. Conditional offers cannot generate a CAS, so any outstanding condition (a final transcript, a language score, a deposit) has to be cleared first.

Two. Pay the deposit your university requires and receive the CAS. It is a reference number rather than a document, valid for six months, and it can only be used once. Check every field on it against your passport the day you receive it, because an error here causes a refusal later and the university must reissue it.

Three. Hold the required funds for 28 consecutive days. The closing balance must be dated no more than 31 days before you apply. This is the single most mechanically enforced rule in the process.

Four. Apply online, no earlier than six months before your course starts if applying from outside the UK, or three months if applying from inside it.

Five. Pay the application fee and the Immigration Health Surcharge for the full length of your permission.

Six. Provide biometrics, either at a visa application centre or through the UK Immigration: ID Check app where your nationality and document type allow it.

Seven. Upload documents and wait. Standard processing outside the UK is around three weeks; inside the UK, around eight. Priority services exist at additional cost where available.

Apply as early as the rules allow rather than as early as you feel ready. Appointment availability at visa centres in high-demand countries is frequently the binding constraint, and it is entirely outside your control.

Documents, and How They Get Applications Refused

Refusals on documentation are more common than refusals on substance, and nearly all of them come from a short list of errors.

Bank statements are the main offender. The account must be in your name, your parent's name, or a legal guardian's name, and no one else's. A statement from an uncle, a sibling, or a family friend does not qualify however genuine the support. If a parent's account is used, you need their written consent plus proof of relationship, usually a birth certificate.

The 28-day rule is applied literally. If the balance dips below the required amount on a single day within that window, the statement fails. Move the money in, then leave it completely alone for a full calendar month before you apply.

Accepted financial evidence includes personal or parental bank statements, a building society passbook, a certificate of deposit held for 28 days, or an official financial sponsorship letter. Not accepted: shares, bonds, pension funds, overdraft facilities, or credit card limits. Some nationalities can rely on the differential evidence arrangement and are not required to submit financial documents upfront, though they must still hold the funds and produce them if asked.

Translations of any document not in English or Welsh must be certified, and must include the translator's credentials, confirmation of accuracy, the date, and contact details. A translation without those elements is treated as no translation at all.

Finally, declare any previous visa refusal by any country, honestly. Undisclosed refusals are routinely discovered and are treated as deception, which carries a ten-year ban. A disclosed refusal is usually survivable; a concealed one is not.

The Credibility Interview

A large proportion of applicants are asked to attend a short credibility interview, usually by video, and it catches out people who assumed the paperwork was the whole test.

The interview is assessing whether you are a genuine student. Typical questions: why this course, why this university, why the UK rather than somewhere closer or cheaper, what the course contains, who is paying and where the money came from, what you intend to do afterwards. It usually lasts ten to twenty minutes.

Answers that cause problems are vague ones. An applicant who cannot name a single module on their own course, or who does not know roughly what the tuition costs, or who gives a different reason for choosing the university than the one in their application, raises exactly the doubt the interview exists to test.

Preparation is straightforward and most people skip it. Read your course page properly and be able to name three modules and one member of staff whose work interests you. Know your tuition fee, your living cost estimate, and the source of the funds. Have a specific answer for why this university rather than a cheaper one, and be ready to explain your plans after graduation in terms that do not amount to staying permanently.

Answer in your own words. Rehearsed answers delivered verbatim sound rehearsed, and interviewers are experienced at spotting a script. Where an agent helped with your application, make sure you can explain everything in it yourself, because the interview is partly designed to detect applications the applicant did not write.

Dependants and Who Can Still Bring Family

The rules here changed sharply and a great deal of outdated advice remains in circulation.

Since January 2024, most international students cannot bring dependants. The exceptions are narrow: students on postgraduate research programmes, meaning PhDs and research-based masters degrees, and students on government-sponsored courses lasting longer than six months.

Taught masters students, including one-year MSc and MA programmes, cannot bring a spouse or children. This is the change that affects the largest number of applicants, and it is not discretionary.

Where dependants are permitted, each one requires their own application, their own Immigration Health Surcharge payment, and additional maintenance funds held under the same 28-day rule. The additional amounts are considerable and are frequently underestimated when families budget.

Dependants of eligible students generally have full work rights, which can materially change household finances. Children can attend state schools without charge.

One point that catches people: a dependant application submitted separately and later is treated on its own merits and can be refused even where the student's visa was granted. Where both are intended, applying at the same time is safer.

Working While You Study

Work rights are attached to your visa and stated on it, and breaching them is one of the few student visa violations that leads to curtailment.

Degree-level students at higher education institutions can normally work 20 hours a week during term time and full time during vacations. Below degree level, the limit is typically 10 hours. The 20 hours is a hard weekly cap, not an average, so 30 hours one week and 10 the next is a breach even though the total matches.

What counts toward the limit includes paid and unpaid work, and any self-employment is prohibited outright, as is working as a professional sportsperson or entertainer. Setting up a business or freelancing while on a student visa is not permitted, a rule that surprises many students with existing online income.

Placement and internship rules differ. Work that forms an assessed part of your course does not count toward the weekly limit, within an overall cap on how much of the course can be a placement.

Employers are required to check and record your permission, and universities monitor attendance and report withdrawals. Students who stop attending are reported, and permission is curtailed.

Realistically, 20 hours at typical part-time rates covers a portion of living costs in a smaller city and a smaller portion in London. It is a supplement, not a funding plan, and visa applications built on expected earnings rather than held funds are refused.

The Costs Nobody Budgets For

The visa itself is a modest part of what the process costs.

The application fee for a student visa from outside the UK is several hundred pounds. The Immigration Health Surcharge is charged at the student rate per year of permission, and permission usually extends a few months beyond the course, which means a one-year masters is typically charged for more than one year. For a three-year undergraduate degree the surcharge alone runs well into four figures and must be paid upfront, in full, at the point of application.

Beyond that: the visa application centre may charge for appointments, courier return of your passport, and document scanning. Priority processing, where offered, is a further several hundred pounds. Certified translations run per page. A tuberculosis test is mandatory for applicants from a long list of countries and must be done at an approved clinic.

Then the costs that are not visa costs at all but arrive at the same time: the tuition deposit, the first rent payment plus a deposit, flights, and initial living expenses before any student account or job exists.

A realistic figure for a student from outside Europe arriving for a one-year masters, excluding tuition, is several thousand pounds before the course begins. Planning against the visa fee alone is how people arrive with no margin.

After Approval: eVisas and Arrival

The physical biometric residence permit has been phased out in favour of a digital immigration status, and this catches out students who arrive expecting a card.

Most students now receive an eVisa, accessed through a UKVI account. You will need to create the account, link your passport, and be able to generate a share code to prove your status to a landlord, an employer, or a university. Do this before you travel, and keep the login details somewhere you will not lose them, because recovering access from abroad is slow.

Where a vignette is placed in your passport for travel, it is valid for a short window, typically 90 days, and you must enter the UK within it. Book flights accordingly; the window does not move.

Carry your CAS, your offer letter, proof of funds, and accommodation details in your hand luggage. Border officers occasionally ask, and having the documents in a checked bag is an avoidable problem.

Register with a GP in the first weeks. You have paid the health surcharge and are entitled to NHS care, and registering when you are already unwell is considerably harder than registering when you are not.

If the Application Is Refused

Refusal is not necessarily the end, and the right response depends entirely on the reason given.

Read the refusal notice carefully; it states the paragraph of the rules relied on. Mechanical refusals (funds held for 27 days rather than 28, a missing translation certificate, an expired CAS) are fixable, and the normal course is to reapply immediately with the defect corrected rather than to appeal. Student visa refusals generally carry no right of appeal, only administrative review where a caseworking error is alleged.

Credibility refusals are harder. A refusal on the basis that you are not a genuine student requires you to address the specific doubt raised, which usually means a stronger explanation of course choice, clearer evidence of the source of funds, and better preparation for a second interview. Reapplying with identical material produces an identical result.

Refusals involving deception are the serious category and carry long re-entry bans. These require proper legal advice rather than a second attempt.

Tell your university immediately in any case. Institutions deal with this constantly, have staff who understand the rules, and can frequently defer your place to the next intake, which removes the pressure to submit a rushed second application. Moving From Student to Work Status

Most students want to know what comes after, and the routes have narrowed but not closed.

The Graduate visa allows you to stay and work without sponsorship after completing an eligible course. It is unsponsored, carries no minimum salary, and permits self-employment, which makes it the most flexible permission most graduates will hold. It cannot be extended and does not count toward settlement, so it is best used as a runway to find a sponsored role rather than as a destination.

The Skilled Worker visa requires a licensed sponsor and a salary above the relevant threshold, with lower thresholds available to recent graduates as new entrants for a limited period. Switching from inside the UK is permitted and avoids a return home.

The Global Talent visa suits researchers and those with a strong record in technology, academia, or the arts, and requires endorsement rather than a job offer.

The practical advice is to begin the job search a long way before graduation, because the Graduate visa clock starts at the end of the course and sponsored recruitment is slow. Students who wait until results are published routinely spend half of their permission applying.
