Chevening Scholarship 2027: Eligibility, the Four Essays, and What Happens at Interview

By Amara DialloPublished September 12, 202615 min read

Chevening is the UK government's international scholarship, funded by the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office. It pays for one year of taught postgraduate study at any UK university, and it is among the most competitive schemes in the world, with tens of thousands of applications for roughly 1,500 awards each cycle.

What separates Chevening from most scholarships is what it selects for. This is not primarily an academic competition. The programme exists to build a network of people who will hold influence in their own countries, and the selection criteria say so openly. Applicants with outstanding grades and no record of leading anything are rejected in large numbers every year. Applicants with unremarkable degrees and a demonstrable track record of moving things forward are selected.

Who Is Eligible

The eligibility rules are applied mechanically, and candidates are removed before anyone reads their essays if they fail one.

  • You must be a citizen of a Chevening-eligible country. The list covers most of Africa, Asia, Latin America, the Caribbean, the Middle East, and parts of Europe, and it changes between cycles.
  • You must hold an undergraduate degree that qualifies you for postgraduate study in the UK.
  • You must have at least 2,800 hours of work experience, which works out at roughly two years of full-time employment. Paid work, voluntary work, internships and self-employment all count, and the application asks you to itemise it.
  • You must apply to three different eligible UK courses and hold an unconditional offer from one of them by the stated deadline, usually in mid-July.
  • You must return to your home country for at least two years after the scholarship ends.

Two exclusions catch people out. Anyone who has previously studied in the UK with UK government funding is ineligible. And employees of the UK government, of Chevening's partner organisations, or their relatives, cannot apply.

The work experience figure is the most common eligibility failure. Count carefully and honestly, include part-time and voluntary work at the correct hourly rate, and do not round upward. The calculation is checked.

What the Award Covers

Chevening pays tuition in full for most taught masters courses, with a cap that applies to a small number of unusually expensive programmes, where you would be liable for the excess. It adds a monthly living allowance set at a higher rate for London than for the rest of the UK, an economy return flight, an arrival allowance, a departure allowance, and the cost of the visa application and the Immigration Health Surcharge.

What it does not cover is a family. Chevening funds one person. Dependants are not funded, and since the 2024 rule change most taught masters students cannot bring dependants to the UK at all. For applicants with children this is frequently the deciding factor, and it is better confronted at the start than discovered in July.

The living allowance is adequate for a single person living modestly and is not generous. London in particular absorbs it quickly, and most scholars supplement it from savings.

The Four Essays

The application asks four questions, each with a 500-word limit that is enforced by the portal. They are the whole application. Two applicants with identical CVs are separated entirely by what they write here.

Leadership and influence

The question asks for an example of your leadership and influence. The failure mode is describing a job title. A manager who lists their responsibilities has not answered it.

What the reader wants is one specific situation where you moved something that would not have moved without you. Set out the problem, what you did, who you had to bring with you, and what changed as a result, with a number attached wherever one exists. Influence without formal authority scores particularly well, because it is harder and it is what the programme is trying to identify.

Networking

This question is widely misread as an invitation to say you enjoy meeting people. It is asking whether you can build and use professional relationships to achieve something.

Give a concrete example: a relationship you built deliberately, what you were trying to accomplish, and what came out of it. Then say specifically how you would use the Chevening network, naming the kinds of people and the purpose rather than saying you look forward to lifelong friendships.

Studying in the UK

Here you explain your three course choices and why the UK. Weak answers praise British education in general terms. Strong answers name modules, name academics whose work is relevant, and explain why these three courses are variations on one coherent objective rather than three unrelated options.

If your three choices look like a scattergun, the reader concludes you have not decided what you want, and that undermines the fourth essay before they reach it.

Career plan

The most heavily weighted essay and the one most often answered badly. Chevening is funded to build influence in your country, so this question is the justification for the whole expenditure.

The failing version is broad ambition with no named actors and no sequence. The version that works names what you will do in the first year after returning, names the organisation or sector, connects it to something already in motion in your career, and states the obstacle you expect and how you intend to work around it. A plan to reform a national system reads as naive. A plan to take a working pilot from two regions to eight reads like someone who has tried to change something.

References and the Offer Requirement

References are only requested if you are shortlisted, which means you should identify them early but not chase them at application stage. You need two, and they must not be relatives. One academic and one professional is the usual combination.

The university offer requirement is the step that eliminates otherwise successful candidates. You must apply to three eligible UK courses yourself, separately from the Chevening application, and hold an unconditional offer from one by the July deadline. Conditional offers do not count.

Start university applications in parallel with the Chevening application, not after the interview. UK admissions can take months, and conditions such as final transcripts or an English test result take time to clear. Candidates who wait for the Chevening result before applying to universities routinely run out of time.

What Happens at Interview

Shortlisted candidates are interviewed at the British embassy or high commission in their own country, usually between February and April. The interview runs around thirty minutes with a panel of two or three, typically including an embassy representative and a Chevening alumnus.

It is competency-based and structured. The panel works through a defined set of areas and scores your answers against a rubric, which means every candidate is asked broadly the same things and improvisation is not rewarded.

Expect to be asked about the leadership example from your essay in more detail, about why these specific courses, about your career plan and how realistic it is, and about how you would use the network. Expect at least one probing follow-up on the weakest part of your plan, because testing that is the point.

Three preparations make the difference. Reread your own application the day before, because months have passed and the panel will quote it back to you. Prepare a two-minute and a thirty-second version of what you do. And have an honest answer ready for what you would do without the scholarship, because "I could not proceed" is a weak answer and describing a slower, harder route shows the plan does not depend on winning.

The question people fumblePanels frequently ask what you would contribute to the Chevening network rather than what you would gain from it. Candidates prepare the second and are caught by the first. Have a specific answer: the expertise, contacts, or perspective you bring that other scholars would find useful.

The Timeline for 2027 Entry

Applications for the 2027/28 cohort open in early August 2026 and close in early November 2026. From there the cycle runs long.

Reading and shortlisting take until roughly February. Interviews run February to April. Conditional results are issued from around June. The university offer deadline falls in mid-July, and final confirmation follows once the offer and references are verified. Courses begin in September and October 2027.

That is fourteen months from opening the portal to starting the degree, and the parts you control are concentrated at the beginning. Everything after November is waiting, apart from the university applications you should be running in parallel.

Why Strong Candidates Get Rejected

A handful of patterns account for most unsuccessful applications from otherwise credible people.

Writing a CV in prose. The essays are not a place to restate your career history. The panel has your work experience. They want the specific situations behind it.

Generic ambition. Wanting to contribute to national development describes every applicant. Naming the mechanism, the organisation, and the first step describes one.

Incoherent course choices. Three courses in three different fields tell the reader your direction is unsettled.

Leaving the university applications until after the interview. Every cycle produces successful candidates who lose the award on the offer deadline.

Treating the return commitment as paperwork. Panels probe it, and an applicant who is visibly planning to remain in the UK is selecting themselves out of a programme built on the opposite premise.

Reapplication is permitted and common, and a meaningful share of each cohort applied before. What changes the result is rarely better writing. It is a stronger year: more responsibility, a measurable outcome, a clearer direction.

Choosing Your Three Courses

The three-course requirement is treated by many applicants as a formality and is one of the clearest signals the panel reads.

Your three choices should look like one objective approached three ways, not three different careers. A candidate applying for public policy at one university, development studies at another, and public administration at a third has told a coherent story. A candidate applying for an MBA, a masters in education, and a masters in data science has told the panel they have not decided what they want.

Choose courses that exist, check the entry requirements against your own record honestly, and confirm the course is eligible. Chevening funds one-year taught masters courses. Part-time courses, distance learning, PhDs, and most courses longer than twelve months are not eligible, and applicants are removed every year for choosing an ineligible programme.

Spread your choices by selectivity. One ambitious, one realistic, one where you comfortably exceed the stated requirements. You only need one unconditional offer, and the July deadline is unforgiving, so the safe choice is what protects the award.

Apply to the universities as early as the portals open, usually from October onward. UK admissions decisions frequently take six to twelve weeks, and clearing conditions such as final transcripts or a language test adds more. Every cycle produces candidates who pass the interview and lose the scholarship because no unconditional offer arrived in time.

Counting Your Work Experience

The 2,800-hour requirement is the most common eligibility failure, and it is worth working through carefully rather than estimating.

Full-time employment counts at the hours you actually worked. Part-time work counts pro rata. Paid and unpaid internships count. Voluntary work counts. Self-employment and freelance work count. Work placements that formed part of a degree count.

What does not count is time spent studying, and this catches out applicants who assume a research degree or a taught programme with a placement year contributes hours.

The application asks you to enter each role with dates and weekly hours, and the portal calculates the total. Enter it accurately. Rounding a 30-hour week up to 40 across three years to clear the threshold is the kind of discrepancy that surfaces at reference stage, and the consequence is worse than being short.

If you fall below 2,800 hours, you are not eligible this cycle and applying anyway wastes the application. Work another year, count properly, and apply next time from a stronger position.

How Chevening Compares to the Alternatives

Applying to Chevening alone is a common and avoidable mistake. Several schemes overlap enough that the same core material serves more than one application.

Commonwealth Scholarships also fund UK study for citizens of Commonwealth countries, with a stronger development focus and nominations through national agencies. Many candidates qualify for both, and the essays differ enough to be worth adapting rather than reusing.

Fulbright funds study in the United States, is administered through binational commissions in your own country, and covers a wider range of disciplines. The processes are independent, so applying to both is straightforward.

DAAD funds study in Germany, weights academic and professional fit more heavily than leadership, and suits candidates whose record is stronger on substance than on visible influence.

Erasmus Mundus funds joint masters across multiple European countries and selects on academic merit and fit with the consortium rather than on leadership.

Your own government's scheme is the most overlooked. National scholarship boards in many countries fund overseas study for citizens, with the competition confined to your own nationals.

The practical point is that a candidate who writes one strong core account of their background, plans, and constraints can adapt it across four or five applications in a single cycle for far less effort than writing four separate ones from scratch.

If You Are Selected

A conditional award in June is not the end of the process, and several candidates stumble in the final stage.

You will need to provide your unconditional offer, your references, and proof that you meet the English requirements of your chosen course by the stated deadlines. Missing any of these can forfeit the award to a reserve candidate.

The visa follows once the award is confirmed. Chevening scholars apply for the Student visa, and the scholarship covers the application fee and the Immigration Health Surcharge. Book your biometric appointment as soon as you are eligible, because appointment availability in high-demand countries is frequently the longest part of the process and is entirely outside your control.

Budget for the gap. The first living allowance payment arrives after you register at your university, which means flights, a rental deposit, and the first few weeks of living costs come out of your own pocket. Plan for four to six weeks of expenses on arrival.

Arrange accommodation before you travel. University halls are the simplest option for a one-year course and they fill quickly once results are released. Private rental in UK cities generally requires a deposit of five weeks' rent plus a guarantor, which international students frequently cannot provide, so check what your university offers first.
