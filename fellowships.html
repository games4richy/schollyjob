Frequently Asked Questions

A scholarship primarily funds study. A fellowship invests in a person. Fellowship applications ask about your character, values, leadership record, and what you are trying to accomplish — not just your academic qualifications. Writing a fellowship application the same way you write a scholarship application is the most common reason strong candidates get rejected.
The acceptance rate runs approximately 2-3% in competitive cycles — comparable to the most selective graduate admissions globally. But unlike academic admissions, selection is not primarily about grades. It is about demonstrated leadership and community impact. Specific, well-documented records of actual work consistently outperform impressive credentials described in generic terms.
Yes. Research fellowships at universities evaluate primarily on academic merit and research potential. YALI Regional Centers are accessible from age 18 and prioritize active community engagement over years of employment. Our full guide on fellowships with no experience covers all accessible entry points.
Many do. Chevening, YALI, Australia Awards, GKS, MEXT, and most government-funded programs have explicit return requirements ranging from 1-2 years. This reflects the funding mandate: investing in people who will contribute to their home country's development. Our essay guides cover how to write a credible return commitment that strengthens rather than weakens your application.
It depends on your career stage and goals. For early-career (18-35): YALI Regional Centers run multiple cohorts per year and are significantly more accessible than annual flagship programs. For mid-career with 3+ years experience: Mandela Washington Fellowship, Chevening, and Commonwealth Scholarships. For researchers: DAAD and TWAS.
