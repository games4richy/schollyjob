DAAD EPOS Scholarships 2027/28: Deadlines, Eligibility, and How to Apply for a Funded Master's in Germany

By Amara DialloPublished September 17, 202614 min read

The DAAD's development-related postgraduate courses programme, known by its German acronym EPOS, is one of the few fully funded master's routes built specifically for working professionals from developing countries. It is also one of the most misunderstood. Every year a large share of applications fail before anyone reads them, because applicants send them to the wrong place, to the wrong course, or two years after they stopped being eligible.

The 2027/28 cycle is open now. Most participating courses close between August and November 2026, and a large number use 15 October 2026 as their deadline for study beginning in October 2027. If you are reading this in September, you have weeks, not months, and the preparation that takes longest has to start immediately.

What EPOS Is, and How It Differs From Other DAAD Awards

Most DAAD scholarships let you choose a German university and apply to the DAAD directly. EPOS works the other way round. The DAAD has selected a fixed list of postgraduate courses at German universities whose subjects matter for development: public health, water management, renewable energy, agricultural economics, urban planning, governance, environmental engineering, and similar fields. Each of those courses receives a quota of DAAD scholarships to award.

That structure has one consequence you cannot ignore. You apply to the course, not to the DAAD. The official DAAD scholarship database states plainly that applications sent to the DAAD will not be forwarded to the course. The course runs its own selection, nominates its preferred candidates, and the DAAD then makes the final award decision.

The full list of eligible courses and their individual deadlines is published by the DAAD as the EPOS list of courses with application deadlines, and the programme conditions are on the DAAD scholarship database entry for EPOS. Read both before anything else. Secondary websites routinely republish last year's deadlines.

Who Is Eligible for 2027/28

The conditions are applied strictly, and several of them eliminate otherwise strong candidates.

  • Citizenship of an eligible country. The DAAD publishes a list of developing and newly industrialised countries. Most of Africa, much of Asia, Latin America, and parts of Eastern Europe and the Middle East are included. Check the current list rather than assuming.
  • A bachelor's degree, normally of four years. Some courses accept three-year degrees combined with further study; the course page will say.
  • At least two years of relevant professional experience after your first degree, completed by the time you apply.
  • A degree that is not too old. Your last degree should generally not be more than six years old at the time of application, meaning October 2026 for this cycle.
  • Strong academic results, typically in the upper third of your graduating class.
  • Language ability in the language of instruction. Many EPOS courses teach in English; others teach in German or both.

The professional experience requirement is the one people misjudge most often. It must be relevant to the course. Two years as a bank teller does not qualify you for a master's in water resources management, however long you worked. The strongest applicants are already working on the problem the course addresses, usually in government, a public utility, an NGO, a research institute, or a development project, and intend to return to that work.

The degree age limit catches mid-career professionals. If you graduated in 2019 and have worked for seven years, you may be the ideal candidate on paper and still fall outside the rule. Check the exact wording on your chosen course's page; a few courses interpret it differently.

What the Scholarship Pays in 2027/28

EPOS is a full scholarship, and the package is more than a monthly stipend.

  • Monthly payment: €992 for master's students and €1,400 for doctoral candidates, the doctoral rate having risen from February 2026.
  • Health, accident and personal liability insurance contributions.
  • A travel allowance for the journey to Germany and back, unless another funder already covers it.
  • A preparatory German language course before the degree begins, usually lasting several months, with the stipend paid during it.
  • Additional allowances in some circumstances, including a study and research allowance and, where eligible, rent and family supplements.

Whether tuition is covered depends on the course. Most EPOS courses are at public universities, where tuition for most programmes is free or low. A minority of courses charge fees; check each course page for how fees are handled for DAAD scholarship holders before you rely on the package.

A realistic word on the money: €992 a month is enough for a single person to live modestly in most German university towns if you get a student dormitory room, and tight in Munich, Frankfurt, Hamburg, or Berlin. Choosing the Right Course

Because you apply to the course, choosing the course is the application's first and most consequential decision.

Start from your work, not from the university's reputation. Read the course descriptions against what you do today and what you want to do on your return. A public health officer running an immunisation programme belongs in an international health or health management course. A water utility engineer belongs in water resources or urban water management. A course that matches your work lets you write an application that makes sense on its own terms. A course that does not forces you to explain the mismatch.

Many EPOS courses let you apply to up to three courses in the same cycle, but each application is separate, and you must indicate your order of preference. Treat this as three distinct applications, each written for that course. A single generic motivation letter sent to three different programmes is easy to spot and rarely succeeds.

Pay attention to the entry requirements on each course page. Some expect a specific first degree. Some require a minimum English score. Some demand extra documents, such as a research proposal or a list of publications. These requirements vary more than applicants expect.

The Documents You Will Need

Requirements differ by course, but most ask for a similar set. The details matter, because incomplete files are rejected without review.

  • The DAAD application form, completed and signed.
  • A CV in Europass format, hand-signed and dated, with your professional experience set out month by month.
  • A motivation letter, hand-signed, normally limited to around two pages, linked to your professional work and your plans after returning.
  • Letters of recommendation, typically at least one from a current employer and often one academic reference. Several courses also require a letter from your employer confirming your role and, ideally, that your job will be available on your return.
  • Certified copies of degree certificates and transcripts, with certified translations if not in English or German.
  • A language certificate, such as IELTS or TOEFL, or proof of German if the course is taught in German.

Two things take longer than any other part of the process. Official transcripts from many universities take four to twelve weeks to issue. And certified translations and copies depend on your local notary or the issuing institution. If your course closes on 15 October and you have not requested transcripts yet, do it today.

If your English test is the obstacle, some EPOS courses accept a medium-of-instruction letter from your university in place of a test. Our guide to master's scholarships without IELTS covers how those letters work and when they are accepted.

Writing the Motivation Letter

This letter carries more weight in EPOS than in most scholarship applications, because the selection committees are assessing development relevance, not academic brilliance alone.

The letter needs to answer four questions, in roughly this order. What problem do you work on, and in what setting? What have you done about it, with at least one concrete result? What specific knowledge or skill are you missing that this course provides? And what will you do with it when you return, in which organisation?

The weakest letters describe a passion for development. The strongest describe a job. "I manage a team of six field staff monitoring groundwater levels across two districts, and we have no way to model recharge rates, which is why seasonal shortages keep surprising us" tells the committee exactly who you are and why this course matters. Name the modules that address your gap. Show that you have read the course's own description.

The return plan deserves specific attention. EPOS exists to strengthen institutions in developing countries, and committees look closely at whether you will return and apply what you learned. An employer letter confirming your post is held open strengthens this more than any paragraph you could write. For the general structure of strong scholarship essays, see our guide to writing a winning scholarship essay.

Sign by handSeveral EPOS courses require a hand-signed CV and motivation letter. A typed name at the bottom of a PDF is not a signature. Print, sign, date, and scan. It sounds trivial, and it removes applications every year.

How Selection Works and When You Will Hear

After the deadline, each course reviews its applications, shortlists candidates, and may interview them, often by video. The course then sends its ranked nominations to the DAAD, which assembles a selection committee to make the final awards.

The timeline is long. For a 15 October 2026 deadline, decisions typically arrive in the first months of 2027, and successful candidates begin the German language course in spring or early summer 2027 before the degree starts in October. The exact dates differ between courses, so note what your course publishes.

If you are invited to interview, prepare as you would for any structured scholarship interview: specific stories, a clear account of your work, and a concrete return plan. Our guide to scholarship interview questions covers the standard question set.

Some courses also admit strong applicants who do not receive a DAAD scholarship, if they can fund themselves. An admission without funding is not the same outcome, and you should not treat it as one unless you can meet the German student visa's proof-of-funds requirement independently.

Common Reasons Applications Fail

Sending the application to the DAAD. It will not be forwarded.

Missing the course's own deadline. Deadlines vary by course, and some close in August or September.

Insufficient or irrelevant experience. Two years is the minimum, not the target, and it must relate to the course.

An old degree. Applicants beyond the six-year limit are removed on eligibility.

A generic letter. Committees read hundreds and recognise a letter written for no course in particular.

Unsigned or uncertified documents. Mechanical failures that are entirely avoidable.

No plan to return. An application that reads as an emigration route is working against the programme's purpose.

After the Award

Winning the scholarship starts a second administrative stage. You will need a German student visa, which EPOS scholarship holders apply for with their award letter as proof of funding, removing the need for a blocked account. Book your visa appointment at the German embassy or consulate as soon as you have the award, because appointment waiting times in several countries run to months. The German Federal Foreign Office publishes current visa requirements and embassy contacts.

Take the language course seriously even if your degree is taught in English. Daily life, housing, and part-time work run in German, and graduates with B1 or B2 German have far wider options afterwards.

A Four-Week Plan If Your Deadline Is 15 October

If you are starting in mid-September, this is tight but achievable, provided you run several tasks in parallel rather than one after another.

Week one. Choose your courses from the official DAAD list and confirm each deadline on the course's own website. Request official transcripts and certified copies from your university the same day, because this is the slowest item. Email your two recommenders with the course names, deadline, and a short summary of the work you did together. Ask your employer for a letter confirming your role, and if possible, that your position will be held for your return.

Week two. Build your Europass CV, listing experience month by month with responsibilities and at least one measurable result per role. Draft the motivation letter for your first-choice course. If you need an English test and do not have a valid result, book the earliest available date now, and check whether your course accepts a medium-of-instruction letter instead.

Week three. Rewrite the motivation letter for each additional course, rather than copying it. Get one person in your field and one person outside it to read the letters. Arrange certified translations of any documents that are not in English or German.

Week four. Collect the recommendation letters and chase any that are late. Print, sign and date the CV and motivation letters, then scan them. Check every document against the course's own checklist, name the files clearly, and submit at least three days before the deadline.

If a document you cannot control, such as a transcript, will not arrive in time, write to the course coordinator before the deadline and ask how they want you to proceed. Courses handle this regularly, and asking early is far better than submitting an incomplete file in silence.

Bringing a Spouse and Children on EPOS

EPOS is designed for mid-career professionals, so many applicants have a spouse or children. The scholarship can help, within limits.

Where eligible, the DAAD pays a family allowance for a spouse and children who accompany the scholarship holder to Germany for a sufficient period, and in some cases a rent subsidy. The conditions and amounts are set out in the scholarship terms you receive with the award, and they change over time, so do not budget around a figure until you have it in writing.

Family members need their own visas for family reunification, with separate applications and appointments. In practice, many scholarship holders arrive alone for the language course and bring their family once they have secured suitable housing, which in larger German cities can take months. Family-sized accommodation is the hardest part of the move, so start the search early and ask the university's international office for help.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there an age limit? The EPOS programme conditions do not set a fixed age limit. The practical limit is the rule that your last degree should not be more than six years old at the time of application.

Can I apply if I am already studying for a master's degree? EPOS funds a full postgraduate degree, and applicants who are already enrolled in, or have completed, an equivalent degree are generally a weak fit. Check the course's eligibility wording carefully before applying.

Can I apply to EPOS and another scholarship in the same year? Yes. Applying to EPOS alongside Chevening, Commonwealth, or national government schemes is common and sensible. If you receive more than one award, you will need to choose, and you should tell the programmes you decline promptly.

Do I need German? Not for English-taught courses. You will, however, take the preparatory German course, and daily life goes far more smoothly with some German.

What happens if my course admits me but the DAAD does not fund me? Some courses offer places to self-funded students. Accept only if you can meet the German student visa's proof-of-funds requirement without the scholarship.
