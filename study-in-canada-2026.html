Study in Canada 2026: Cheapest Universities Accepting International Applications Now

By Amara DialloPublished June 17, 2026⏳ 12 min read

Canada's reputation for being expensive is accurate for a specific version of Canada: the 20-university, Toronto-or-Vancouver version that most international students default to without investigating the alternatives. International students who have attended smaller universities in provinces outside Ontario and British Columbia consistently report living costs and tuition levels that are considerably lower than the headline figures suggest, alongside genuine quality of life and real post-graduation pathways. The question is not whether Canada is affordable. It is whether the version of Canada being targeted is the right one for each applicant's budget and goals.

The Cheapest Canadian Universities for International Students in 2026

Memorial University of Newfoundland - St. John's, NL

Annual international tuition: approximately CAD $11,460 for most undergraduate programs. This is the lowest among major Canadian universities. Memorial is a full research university with strong programs in ocean engineering, natural resources, medicine, and business. St. John's is one of Canada's most affordable cities to live in, with average student housing costs appreciably below Toronto or Vancouver. Application deadlines vary by program - most fall intake applications close March 1. Apply at MUN.

University of Regina - Regina, SK

Annual international tuition: approximately CAD $18,000–$22,000 depending on program. Regina is one of Canada's most affordable cities with housing costs well below the national average. The University of Regina has strong programs in engineering, business, and social work. Saskatchewan's Provincial Nominee Program (SINP) is one of Canada's most accessible provincial immigration pathways, which matters if you're thinking beyond your degree. Apply at U of R.

Cape Breton University - Sydney, NS

Annual international tuition: approximately CAD $18,000–$23,000. Cape Breton has become notably popular among international students because of Nova Scotia's Active Pathways immigration program, which has historically offered post-graduation immigration pathways with lower requirements than the federal stream. The Atlantic region of Canada broadly has been actively recruiting international students as a population strategy. Apply at CBU.

Understanding Canadian Student Visas in 2026

A Canadian study permit requires a letter of acceptance from a Designated Learning Institution (DLI), proof of financial support (typically CAD $20,635 for one year as of 2026, covering tuition plus living costs), a valid passport, and a biometric appointment. Processing times vary by country - currently averaging four to eight weeks for most nationalities. Apply through IRCC's online portal.

One change in 2026 worth knowing: Canada tightened its international student permit rules in 2024 and 2025 in response to overcrowding concerns at certain institutions. Some colleges and universities now require a provincial attestation letter before IRCC will process your study permit. This has not considerably affected university-level applicants, but if you're considering a college or diploma program, verify that your institution still has attestation letters available for international students.

Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)

The Post-Graduation Work Permit is the main reason many international students choose Canada over other English-speaking destinations. After completing a program of at least eight months at a DLI, you can apply for a PGWP that allows you to work full-time in Canada for a period equal to the length of your program, up to three years. A three-year PGWP from a two-year Master's program is a three-year open work permit - you can work for any employer in any occupation. That's the pathway to permanent residency via Canadian work experience.

Scholarships Available Specifically for International Students in Canada

Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarships offer CAD $50,000/year for three years for doctoral students - highly competitive but among the most prestigious in the world. The Ontario Graduate Scholarship provides CAD $15,000/year for Ontario university students. Most major Canadian universities have entrance scholarships for international students ranging from CAD $2,000–$30,000. The University of Toronto's International Scholars Program provides CAD $35,000 annually - competitive but worth applying to. The key thing most students don't do: apply for internal university scholarships at the time of admission. These are automatic consideration at some universities, but require separate applications at others. Ask explicitly during your application process.

Why Canada Is a Smart Study Destination in 2026

Canada's combination of world-class universities, manageable tuition costs relative to the United States and United Kingdom, welcoming immigration policies, a stable and multicultural society, and post-graduation work permit pathways makes it one of the most strategically sensible study destinations in the world for international students in 2026. Unlike the US, which has a notoriously unpredictable skilled worker visa system, Canada explicitly uses international student graduation and Canadian work experience as pathways toward permanent residency.

The Post-Graduation Work Permit allows international graduates to work in Canada for up to three years after completing a degree program of two years or longer. During that time, Canadian work experience contributes to eligibility under the Canadian Experience Class pathway of the Express Entry immigration system. For international students who want to build a career in a stable, high-income country, the Canadian pathway is more reliably accessible than any other high-income English-speaking destination in 2026.

The Major Canadian Universities and What They Offer

University of Toronto | McGill University | University of British Columbia | University of Waterloo | McMaster University | University of Alberta

The University of Toronto ranks among the top 25 universities globally by most measures. Its international tuition for graduate programs ranges from approximately 16,000 to 45,000 Canadian dollars per year depending on the program. The university has over 150 graduate research programs, one of the largest graduate research ecosystems in North America, and is located in Toronto, Canada's largest and most economically diverse city with exceptional employment prospects for graduates.

McGill University in Montreal is Canada's most internationally recognized university by global reputation and is the oldest university in Quebec. Graduate international tuition is generally lower than Toronto, partly because Quebec has historically maintained lower university fees. Montreal's lower cost of living compared to Toronto and Vancouver makes McGill an excellent value proposition for quality research. The city is bilingual, vibrant, and markedly more affordable than most major North American academic hubs.

The University of British Columbia in Vancouver consistently ranks among the top 50 universities globally. Vancouver's quality of life is exceptional but its cost of living is among the highest in Canada. Students targeting UBC need to budget accordingly. The university has strong programs in engineering, computer science, forestry, medicine, and business. International graduate tuition typically ranges from 8,000 to 40,000 Canadian dollars annually.

The University of Waterloo has one of the most highly regarded computer science and engineering programs in the world, with a cooperative education system that places students in paid work terms at companies including Google, Microsoft, Apple, and dozens of major technology firms. For technical fields, Waterloo's co-op placements provide both work experience and income during the degree, which sharply reduces the actual net cost of the program.

Funding Your Canadian Degree

Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarships | Trudeau Foundation Doctoral Scholarships | Provincial awards | University-specific funding | External scholarships

The Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarships fund 50,000 Canadian dollars per year for three years for doctoral students demonstrating academic excellence, research potential, and leadership skills. International students studying in Canada on Canadian government funding are eligible. This is among the most generous doctoral funding available in Canada for international students and is competitive but open.

Most Canadian graduate research programs provide teaching assistant or research assistant funding that covers tuition plus a living stipend. Students in funded PhD programs at major Canadian research universities often pay little or no net tuition after funding packages are factored in. Before applying to any Canadian PhD program, email the potential supervisor directly to ask whether their research group has funding available for international students, because this determines whether the program is financially feasible for you. Supervisors who have active grants can often fund strong international students regardless of whether a formal scholarship exists.

Provincial scholarships vary by province. Ontario Graduate Scholarships fund up to 15,000 Canadian dollars for one year for graduate students at Ontario universities. British Columbia, Alberta, and Quebec all have provincial graduate funding that international students enrolled in those provincial universities can access in many cases. Check each university's graduate financial aid page for current provincial funding available to international students in their province.

Student Visa and Application Process

International students need a Canadian study permit to study at a Canadian institution for programs longer than six months. The study permit application requires a letter of acceptance from a Designated Learning Institution, proof of financial support covering tuition plus living costs (approximately 20,635 Canadian dollars for the first year plus tuition), a valid passport, and a biometrics enrollment appointment at a local service point. Processing times for study permits vary by country of origin: from two weeks for low-traffic nationalities to twelve weeks or more for higher-traffic ones. Apply as early as possible after receiving your letter of acceptance.

Students from countries included in Canada's Student Direct Stream can benefit from faster study permit processing. The SDS currently includes students from China, India, Philippines, Vietnam, Morocco, Pakistan, Senegal, Antigua and Barbuda, Brazil, Colombia, Costa Rica, Peru, and Trinidad and Tobago. SDS applicants who have paid their first-year tuition in full before applying, have a GIC (Guaranteed Investment Certificate) of at least 10,000 Canadian dollars, and meet the English language requirements can get permit decisions in as little as 20 business days.

Cost of Living and Working While Studying

Canadian cities vary considerably in cost. Toronto and Vancouver are among the most expensive cities in North America for housing. Montreal is notably more affordable, and smaller university cities like Waterloo, Kingston, Halifax, and London Ontario are a great deal cheaper than the major metropolitan centers. When choosing a Canadian university, factor in the monthly rent for a one-bedroom apartment in each city: approximately 2,200 to 2,800 Canadian dollars in Toronto, 2,500 to 3,200 in Vancouver, 1,400 to 1,900 in Montreal, and 1,000 to 1,400 in smaller university cities.

International students studying in Canada on a valid study permit can work up to 24 hours per week off-campus during academic sessions and full-time during scheduled breaks. At minimum wage rates (15 to 17 Canadian dollars per hour depending on the province), 20 hours per week provides approximately 1,300 to 1,400 Canadian dollars per month before tax, which covers a meaningful portion of living costs. For students in co-op programs like Waterloo, work term income is considerably higher, often 2,500 to 5,000 Canadian dollars per month.

The Study Permit Application, Step by Step

Canada's process has more moving parts than most, and several of them are recent additions that older guidance omits entirely.

One. Receive a letter of acceptance from a designated learning institution. Only institutions on the official DLI list can support a study permit, and the list changes, so verify yours rather than assuming.

Two. Obtain a provincial or territorial attestation letter. Since 2024 most study permit applications require one, issued by the province where you will study, and institutions are allocated a limited number. Exemptions apply to masters and doctoral applicants and to some other categories.

Three. Pay the first year's tuition, or a large portion, and obtain a receipt. Applications showing tuition paid are consistently stronger.

Four. Demonstrate financial capacity. The required amount for living costs outside Quebec was raised sharply and now sits above CAD $20,000 for a single applicant for one year, in addition to tuition and travel. This figure is reviewed annually and has moved upward repeatedly.

Five. Complete a medical examination if required for your country of residence or intended activity, and obtain police certificates where requested.

Six. Apply online, provide biometrics, and submit a letter of explanation. Processing times vary widely by country, from a few weeks to several months.

Applicants from participating countries should check eligibility for the Student Direct Stream, which offers faster processing in exchange for a Guaranteed Investment Certificate, upfront tuition payment, and specified language scores.

The Caps, and What They Mean for Applicants

Canada introduced a national cap on new study permit applications in 2024 and has reduced the target further since, which has changed the environment markedly.

Practically, this means three things. Attestation letters are finite and institutions distribute them on their own timelines, so an early application to your chosen institution matters more than it used to. Approval rates have fallen at the college level more than at the university level. And several provinces have prioritised programmes aligned with labour market needs when allocating letters.

Graduate students are treated more favourably. Masters and doctoral applicants have been exempted from the attestation requirement in most cases and face fewer restrictions on post-graduation work rights, which has shifted the calculation toward university study rather than shorter college programmes for many applicants.

The post-graduation work permit rules also changed. Graduates of public colleges in fields not linked to identified labour shortages have seen eligibility restricted, while university degree graduates broadly retain access. Anyone choosing a programme primarily as an immigration route should verify current post-graduation work permit eligibility for that specific credential before enrolling, because the rules have moved more than once and enrolment decisions made on 2023 information are now frequently wrong.

Private college partnerships delivering public college curricula lost eligibility entirely. Avoid them.

Why Study Permits Get Refused

Canadian refusal rates for some countries are high, and the reasons given are consistent enough to prepare against.

Insufficient ties to your home country. The officer must be satisfied you will leave at the end of your authorised stay. Evidence that helps: family responsibilities, property, an employer holding a position for you, or a clear professional reason to return. This is the most common single ground for refusal and the least addressed by applicants.

Unclear purpose of study. An applicant who already holds a masters applying for a two-year diploma in an unrelated field invites the conclusion that study is not the real purpose. Where your programme represents a change of direction or a step back in level, explain why explicitly in the letter of explanation rather than leaving the officer to guess.

Financial capacity not established. Funds that appeared recently, are held by a distant relative, or cannot be traced to a legitimate source are treated as unproven. Show the origin of the money, not only its presence.

Documents inconsistent. Names spelled differently across documents, dates that do not reconcile, and employment letters that contradict a CV all generate doubt.

Where a refusal occurs, request the officer's notes through an access to information request. They state the actual reasoning, which is usually more specific than the refusal letter, and they are the only reliable basis for a stronger second application. Working During and After Study

Work rights are one of the main attractions and they have been adjusted recently.

Students enrolled full time at a designated learning institution in an eligible programme may work off campus during term, with the weekly limit raised from the long-standing 20 hours to 24 hours. Full-time work is permitted during scheduled breaks. On-campus work carries no hourly limit.

Co-op and internship placements forming a required part of the curriculum need a separate co-op work permit, applied for alongside or after the study permit. Programmes with mandatory placements should be identified early so this is not discovered in the final semester.

Spouses of students retain open work permits in a narrowed set of circumstances, principally where the student is in a masters, doctoral, or specified professional programme. Spouses of undergraduate and most college students no longer qualify, a change that has markedly affected family planning for applicants.

The post-graduation work permit remains the central benefit: up to three years for graduates of eligible programmes of two years or longer, and shorter for shorter programmes. It is open, requiring no employer sponsorship, and the Canadian work experience it produces is what feeds directly into the permanent residence routes.

From Study Permit to Permanent Residence

Canada is unusual in designing an explicit path from international study to settlement, and understanding the sequence changes how you choose a programme.

The usual route runs: study permit, graduate, post-graduation work permit, accumulate at least one year of skilled Canadian work experience, then apply through the Canadian Experience Class within Express Entry. Canadian education and Canadian work experience both add points, which is why graduates typically score well above applicants from abroad with similar profiles.

French-speaking graduates have a marked advantage. Category-based draws targeting French-language proficiency have repeatedly had cut-offs far below general rounds, and a graduate with strong French can be invited at a score that would not be competitive otherwise.

Provincial nominee streams specifically for international graduates exist in most provinces and frequently have lower requirements than the federal route, particularly outside Ontario and British Columbia. A graduate of a Saskatchewan or Manitoba institution who intends to stay in that province often has the shortest path available.

Two timing points. The post-graduation work permit is issued once in a lifetime and cannot be extended, so the year of qualifying work experience must be obtained within it. And the work must be at a skill level that counts, which rules out some of the jobs graduates take first.

Health Coverage and Arriving

Health care is provincial rather than national, and coverage for international students varies by where you land.

British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, and Newfoundland extend provincial coverage to international students, generally with a waiting period of up to three months during which private insurance is required. Ontario, Quebec, and several others exclude international students from provincial plans, and universities there enrol students in a mandatory private scheme, typically CAD $600 to $900 annually.

Confirm which applies before arrival and hold private cover for any gap. An uninsured emergency in Canada is expensive enough to end a degree.

On arrival, carry your letter of acceptance, port of entry letter, proof of funds, and accommodation details in hand luggage. The study permit itself is issued at the border rather than before travel, and the officer will ask for these.

Apply for a Social Insurance Number in the first weeks, since no legal employment is possible without one. Open a bank account early, as several banks offer student packages with no monthly fee.

Budget for the first two months. Deposits, furniture, winter clothing, and a phone plan arrive together before any income, and students who plan only for tuition and rent consistently find the first term harder than expected.
