Commonwealth Scholarships 2027/28: Deadlines, Nominating Agencies, and How to Apply From Your Country

By Amara DialloPublished September 22, 202614 min read

The Commonwealth Scholarship is the UK's other major government-funded award, and it is the one built for people who could not otherwise pay to study abroad. That condition is written into the eligibility rules, which makes it a different competition from Chevening and a better fit for many of the applicants who assume Chevening is their only option.

The 2027/28 round is open now and closes at 16:00 BST on 20 October 2026. Nominating bodies then have until December 2026 to submit their chosen candidates to the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission. If you are reading this in late September, the binding constraint is not the essay. It is your nominating agency's own internal deadline, which is frequently earlier than the CSC's.

The Awards, and Which One You Should Be Applying To

The Commonwealth Scholarship Commission runs several distinct schemes, and applicants regularly apply to the wrong one.

  • Commonwealth Master's Scholarships. A full one-year taught master's in the UK, for candidates from least developed and lower middle income Commonwealth countries. This is the main award most people mean.
  • Commonwealth PhD Scholarships. Full doctoral funding in the UK, also for least developed and lower middle income countries.
  • Commonwealth Shared Scholarships. Master's funding delivered jointly with specific UK universities, for candidates who would not otherwise be able to study in the UK. You apply directly to the participating university, not through a nominating agency, which makes this the most accessible route for many people.
  • Commonwealth Distance Learning Scholarships. A UK master's studied from home, with no relocation. Far less competitive and badly underused.
  • Commonwealth Professional Fellowships. Short placements with a UK host organisation, applied for by the host rather than by you.

Read the scheme descriptions on the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission site before choosing. If your country is not eligible for the Master's scheme, you may still be eligible for Shared Scholarships or Distance Learning, and those go under-applied every cycle.

Who Can Apply for a Commonwealth Scholarship

The conditions for the Master's and PhD schemes are close to identical.

  • Be a citizen of, or have been granted refugee status by, an eligible Commonwealth country, or be a British Protected Person, and be permanently resident in an eligible country.
  • Hold, by the start of the award, at least an upper second class (2:1) undergraduate degree. A lower second (2:2) is acceptable where you also hold a relevant postgraduate qualification, usually a master's.
  • Be available to begin your studies in the UK by the start of the academic year in September 2027.
  • Be unable to afford to study in the UK without this scholarship.

That last condition is the one that distinguishes this award, and you should take it at face value. The CSC asks about your financial circumstances and means it. Candidates who could self-fund are not the intended recipients.

The degree classification rule is applied strictly. If your university does not use the UK classification system, your grades will be mapped onto it, and a mid-range result from a system that grades harshly can still convert to a 2:2. If you are close to the line, the postgraduate qualification route is the way in.

What the Scholarship Pays

The Master's award is comprehensive, and the stipend is among the more generous of the UK schemes.

  • Full tuition, settled between the CSC and your university. Scholars pay no part of the fee.
  • A monthly living allowance of around £1,712, rising to roughly £2,000 for scholars at London institutions. Rates are reviewed, so confirm the figure for your cohort.
  • Return airfare from your home country, though not for dependants, and not for journeys made before the award is confirmed.
  • A warm clothing allowance and an arrival allowance.
  • A thesis or study travel grant where your course requires it.
  • Child allowance of around £622 a month for a first child and £154 for a second and third child under 16, available to scholars who are widowed, divorced, or a single parent.

Note what is missing. A spouse's costs are not covered, and since the 2024 rule change most taught master's students cannot bring dependants to the UK at all. Our guide to UK student visa requirements sets out the current dependant rules and what the visa itself costs.

Find Your Nominating Agency First

This is the step that decides whether your application exists at all. For the Master's and PhD schemes, the CSC does not accept direct applications. You must be nominated by one of three routes:

  1. Your country's national nominating agency, usually a ministry of education, a scholarships secretariat, a public service commission, or a similar body.
  2. An approved non-governmental organisation or charitable body.
  3. A selected UK university, for the PhD scheme in particular, where universities nominate their own candidates.

The CSC publishes the nominating body for each country on its website. Find yours today, then find its own deadline and its own additional requirements, which frequently include a separate form, certified documents, and sometimes an interview. Agencies commonly close two to six weeks before the CSC deadline, and some require applications by post or in person.

Every cycle produces candidates who complete the CSC's online form beautifully and are never nominated, because they did not contact the agency that actually forwards applications. Do that part first and the writing second.

Apply to universities in parallelYou need a place on a UK course to take up the award. Start those applications now rather than waiting for a scholarship result in spring. UK admissions commonly take six to twelve weeks, and clearing conditions such as final transcripts or an English test adds more. The Chevening guide explains the same trap in more detail, and it costs applicants places every year.

The Application Itself

You complete the CSC's own online application system, and your nominating agency accesses it. Expect to provide:

  • Personal and citizenship details, and your residency status.
  • Full academic history with transcripts and degree certificates.
  • Your employment history.
  • Up to three proposed UK courses, with a justification for each.
  • A development impact statement, which is the core of the application.
  • A plan of study or, for PhD applicants, a research proposal.
  • References, usually two, submitted through the system.

The development impact statement

This is where Commonwealth applications are won and lost. The CSC's mandate is development, and it selects candidates whose study will contribute to their home country's development needs.

The failing version describes an ambition to help your country develop. The version that works follows a chain: a specific development problem, your current work on it, the capability you lack, the course that supplies it, and what you will do differently in the first year after you return, naming the organisation and the mechanism.

Be concrete about scale. "I will improve healthcare in Nigeria" is not a plan. "I will take the district-level maternal referral protocol we piloted in two LGAs and build the monitoring framework needed to extend it across the state, working through the state primary healthcare board where I already sit on the technical committee" is a plan, and it tells the committee you have thought about the obstacles.

Where you have an employer who will hold your post, say so and attach evidence. Continuity strengthens the return argument more than anything you can assert.

The plan of study

Explain why these specific courses, at these specific universities. Name modules. Name academics whose work connects to your problem. Explain why a UK course rather than one at home, which is a fair question and one that applicants often leave unanswered.

Our guide to writing a winning scholarship essay covers the structures that work across these applications, and the editing pass that removes the padding.

References

You need referees who can speak to both your academic capacity and your professional contribution. Give them at least a month, the scheme details, and a summary of what you are claiming in the application, so their letters reinforce the same picture.

Referees submit through the CSC system directly, and applications with missing references are not forwarded. Chase politely a week before the deadline. Our guide to getting strong letters of recommendation covers who to ask and what to send them.

The Timeline for 2027/28

Now to 20 October 2026: identify your nominating agency, meet its internal deadline, and complete the CSC application.

By December 2026: nominating bodies submit their candidates to the CSC.

Early 2027: the CSC assesses nominated applications. Some nominating agencies interview at their own stage; the CSC itself generally does not interview for the Master's scheme.

Spring to summer 2027: results issued. Successful candidates confirm their university place, then apply for the student visa.

September 2027: courses begin.

Budget for the gap between arrival and the first stipend payment. Flights are reimbursed rather than advanced in many cases, and the first allowance arrives after registration, so plan for four to six weeks of your own funds.

Commonwealth or Chevening?

Many applicants qualify for both, and the two reward different profiles.

Chevening selects on leadership and networking, requires at least 2,800 hours of work experience, and is open to a wider range of countries. It does not assess financial need.

Commonwealth selects on academic merit and development impact, sets no minimum work experience for the Master's scheme, and explicitly targets candidates who cannot otherwise afford UK study. It is restricted to eligible Commonwealth countries.

If you are early in your career with strong grades and a clear development focus, Commonwealth is often the better fit. If you have years of leadership behind you and a visible public profile, Chevening may suit you more. Applying to both in the same cycle is sensible, and much of the core material carries across, though the emphasis in the essays must genuinely change.

Why Applications Fail

Missing the nominating agency. The most common failure of all, and entirely avoidable.

A vague development case. Committees read thousands of statements about contributing to national development. Specificity is the whole differentiator.

Ignoring the financial need condition. Applications that read as though the candidate has other options are competing against the scheme's purpose.

Course choices that do not match the stated plan. Three unrelated courses signal that the direction is unsettled.

Late or missing references. Incomplete applications are not forwarded.

Leaving university applications until after the result. Without an offer, the award cannot be taken up.

If You Are Not Eligible or Not Selected

Check the Shared Scholarships route, which is applied for directly through participating UK universities and reaches candidates the main scheme does not. Check Distance Learning, which removes the visa, the relocation, and most of the cost, and which receives a fraction of the applications.

Beyond the UK, the DAAD EPOS scholarship serves a similar audience of working professionals from developing countries, with deadlines in the same window. For doctoral funding specifically, see our guide to fully funded PhD programmes, which covers the studentship and assistantship routes that fund far more people than named scholarships do.

The Two Routes Most Applicants Overlook

If your country is not eligible for the main Master's scheme, or if you cannot realistically leave your job and family for a year, two other Commonwealth schemes deserve a serious look. Both are consistently under-applied.

Commonwealth Shared Scholarships fund a full-time UK master's jointly with a specific participating university. The crucial difference is the application route: you apply directly to the university, not through a national nominating agency. That removes the single biggest administrative obstacle in the main scheme. The CSC publishes the list of participating universities and courses each cycle, and the awards are targeted at candidates who could not otherwise afford UK study. If your nominating agency is unresponsive or its internal deadline has already passed, check this list first.

Commonwealth Distance Learning Scholarships fund a UK master's studied from your own country, delivered by a UK university online or through a local partner. You keep your job, you keep your income, your family is not disrupted, there is no visa, and there is no relocation cost. The award covers tuition and, in some cases, a contribution toward other costs.

The competition here is far lighter than for the residential award, for reasons that have more to do with prestige than with value. For a mid-career professional whose employer will not hold a post open for a year, a distance learning master's from a UK university is often the better outcome, not the consolation prize.

Eligibility for both follows the same broad pattern as the main scheme, including the requirement that you could not otherwise afford the study. Check the current country lists, because they differ between schemes.

A Four-Week Plan to 20 October

If you are starting in late September, the sequence matters more than the hours you put in.

Week one. Identify your national nominating agency on the CSC website and contact it the same day. Ask for its internal deadline and any additional forms. This single step determines whether the rest is worth doing. In parallel, shortlist up to three UK courses and check their entry requirements against your degree classification.

Week two. Approach your referees with the scheme criteria, your draft statement, and a reminder of your work together. Request official transcripts if you do not already hold them, since university registries are slow. Start the university applications themselves, because you will need an offer by the summer.

Week three. Draft the development impact statement and the plan of study. Write the impact statement first and let the course choices follow from it, rather than the reverse. Have someone in your sector read it and tell you whether the plan is plausible.

Week four. Revise, complete every field of the CSC application, confirm your referees have submitted, and send the file to your nominating agency ahead of its deadline. Keep copies of everything.

If your nominating agency's deadline has already passed for this cycle, do not abandon the year. Apply to Shared Scholarships directly, and set a calendar reminder for August 2027 so that next cycle you begin two months earlier.

Reapplication is permitted, and a stronger year behind you changes the application more than better writing does. If you reach an interview at your nominating agency, our guide to scholarship interview questions covers how structured panels score answers.
